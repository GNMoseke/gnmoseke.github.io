# Tangible (Or Tangible Bytes)
One of my main goals this year is to drop Spotify. I've been subscribed to it for all of my adult
life, plus a few years free as a teenager. Over a decade at this point. That means that I've paid
Spotify about **$800** (by my estimate, and that's conservative, plus it includes a solid 4 years of a
half-price student subscription). Regardless of your opinions on Spotify as a business model (my own
are mixed), that's a lot of fucking money (and they're bumping prices again in June). I feel like I
got my money's worth, but the more I've been thinking about it, and especially as my music taste has
evolved, the more I've come to 2 major sticking points:
1. Most of the music I listen to at this point is from smaller artists. I want to support them
   directly.
2. If Spotify disappears tomorrow, I can never play any of that music again. This is true of every
   SaaS on the planet (duh), but we just take it as a given that the thing will work whenever we
need it.

I generally dislike [DRM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_rights_management). I encourage
you to read [defective by design](https://www.defectivebydesign.org/), because I agree with the
issue it raises, and I think it's important to discuss. That said, I also understand why it's
around. I don't think it's a simple black-and-white issue (though I do fall pretty definitively on
the "DRM bad" side, just for the record), and I'm neither qualified enough nor do I have the energy
to fully dive into the debate in this little post. Anyway, that's enough of that sidebar, but it's
important as a piece of background for (2) above.

So, instead of Spotify, I've started to buy all of my music directly from the artist. Usually this
is via [Bandcamp](https://bandcamp.com/) (which I love), and occasionally it's directly from their
website if I can find it there instead, or from Steam in the case of game soundtracks. I also have a
few of my personal favorite albums on vinyl, just because I like the aesthetic (I hate that I wrote
that but it's true) - I don't have a turntable, I just enjoy being able to give those things that
really hit for me the extra money, and get a nice, physical display piece in return.

I'm only just starting, but currently I have a little over 100 albums, plus a smattering of singles
and a few podcasts that are available for download directly. That may sound like a lot of money.
After all, most albums on Bandcamp go for somewhere around $5-10 (in various currencies, but they all
even out to about that). But, that's not true for everyone. For example, [Autumn
Orange](https://autumnorange.bandcamp.com/music), one of my favorite lofi artists, sells his entire
discography for ~$43. My favorite band, [King Gizzard and the Lizard Wizard](https://kinggizzard.bandcamp.com/),
sell their stuff for about 10AUD per album, which has a really favorable exchange rate to euros. To
be clear, I'm not doing anything to "game the system" here - I'm just giving my money directly to the
artist. That also means I can (usually) get music in multiple formats - `flac` for my desktop, and
high bitrate `mp3` for my phone, which I play using [VLC on
iOS](https://www.videolan.org/vlc/download-ios.html) (which is really excellent at this point).

Is this more work than just forking over my monthly fee to Spotify? Absolutely. Does it have the
same *vastness* of music to listen to? Of course not. But it means that I can *see* the music I own.
I can move it around, back it up, organize it how I want, go crazy with tagging if I'm really feeling
adventurous. And it means that I'm engaging with the music I'm listening to more and more as well.
Rather than just tossing on background noise and zoning out, I'm sitting with some of my favorite
albums, and listening to them again like it's the first time. Actually hearing the lyrics, and how
they make me feel. And until I started doing that again, I didn't realize how special of on
experience it was.

I think it's only fair that I leave off this post with a short list of some of the music that I most
love (and now own)! So, in no particular order, here's a bunch of stuff you should check out:
* [Mattstagraham](https://mattstagraham.bandcamp.com/), from my own hometown! My personal favorite
song is [Caffeine](https://mattstagraham.bandcamp.com/track/caffeine).
* The aforementioned [King Gizzard and the Lizard Wizard](https://kinggizzard.bandcamp.com/). You
can't go wrong with [Butterfly 3000](https://kinggizzard.bandcamp.com/album/butterfly-3000), but my
favorite album of theirs is [Omnium Gatherum](https://kinggizzard.bandcamp.com/album/omnium-gatherum).
* [Beast in Black](https://beastinblack.bandcamp.com/music), [Unleash the
Archers](https://unleashthearchers.bandcamp.com/), and [Brothers of
Metal](https://brothersofmetal.bandcamp.com/music), my current holy trinity of cheesy power metal.
* Again, mentioned above, [Autumn Orange](https://autumnorange.bandcamp.com/music), a wonderful lofi
  artist with a lot of Critical Role themed stuff in particular for extra nerd points.
* [Tom Cardy](https://tomcardy.bandcamp.com/), an aussie with no right to make as many catchy comedy
  songs as he does.
* Any [Supergiant soundtrack](https://store.steampowered.com/developer/supergiantgames/list/35195/)
  (I've written about my love for Supergiant already in my [2024 game
recap](https://purpleshirt.dev/posts/2024-game-recap/)). If I had to choose one, though, the Pyre
soundtrack has made my cry multiple times.
* [Bones UK](https://sumerianrecords.bandcamp.com/album/bones-uk) by the artist of the same name,
and their other album [Soft](https://sumerianrecords.bandcamp.com/album/soft).

Give any and all of those a listen, and if any of it strikes you, consider buying the album directly
rather than streaming it on Spotify or Apple Music or any of the other SaaS DRM music providers.
Happy listening.
